How to use ProofLoop

ProofLoop runs user tests from inside Claude Code or Cursor. You plan the study, run moderated or unmoderated sessions, and get findings where every quote and count is checked against what participants actually said and did. This guide walks one study from install to report.

  • Recordings stay on your laptop or company drive
  • Personal details are redacted before the AI reads anything
  • You approve every finding before it reaches the report

You drive ProofLoop by talking to Claude. Each step shows what to say, which skill runs, and what you get. Your own words work too: the skill is picked from what you ask.

  1. Install the plugin

    You need Node.js 20 or later, and FFmpeg for recordings. whisper.cpp is optional, for transcribing calls on your laptop.

    Terminal (macOS)
    brew install node ffmpeg

    Then in Claude Code, add the plugin from GitHub:

    Claude Code
    /plugin marketplace add minhnguyenrenol/proofloop
/plugin install proofloop@proofloop

    In Cursor, add the plugin from the GitHub repo minhnguyenrenol/proofloop. It brings the same skills, tools and privacy hooks.

  2. Set up your research folder

    Skill: research-setup

    Say to Claude
    Set up ProofLoop for this project.

    Claude asks a few questions, one at a time:

    • Where recordings should live: a folder on this laptop, or a company drive folder.
    • Whether this is a company-managed device with disk encryption, and whether the folder is backed up.
    • Whether you'll run unmoderated tests. Those need the short relay: a company bucket that holds uploads for 24 hours.

    You get a research/ folder with a .gitignore that keeps participant data out of git.

  3. Add your product context

    Skill: add-context

    Say to Claude
    Add product context from the docs in ./product-docs.

    Point it at your product docs (PDF, Word, slides, Markdown), or answer a short guided form. It writes your vision, problem statement, personas and journeys into one page. Every line names its source, and anything missing says "Not provided" instead of being invented.

    You get context.md and a readable context.html to check and edit.

  4. Plan the study

    Skill: plan-study

    Say to Claude
    Plan an unmoderated test of this Figma prototype:
https://www.figma.com/proto/…
Goal: find out whether people can open a savings account.

    Give it a prototype link (Figma, a GitHub-hosted build, or any HTTPS page) and your goal. It drafts research questions, tasks, the consent text and, for moderated tests, a discussion guide. A checker flags leading wording and tasks that give away the answer.

    You get study.yaml, consent.md and guide.md. Read them and ask for changes.

  5. Run the sessions

    Skill: run-session · build-runner

    Moderated, on a call

    Say "Run a moderated session with P1." Claude records consent, then opens the Studio in your browser. It records your Zoom, Meet or Teams window and your mic as separate tracks, with guide sections and notes as you go. Press Stop when the call ends.

    Unmoderated, on a link

    Say "Build the participant page and links for P1 to P5." Host the page on your company site, then send each person their own link. They consent, try the tasks on the prototype and answer short questions while their screen and voice are recorded.

    Nothing records without consent. Each participant link works on one device; if someone has to switch, ask Claude to re-create their link with the device released.

  6. Bring the sessions in

    Skill: import-sessions

    Say to Claude
    Import the finished sessions.

    Unmoderated sessions come down from the relay into your study folder and are deleted from the relay. For calls recorded elsewhere, give Claude the recording and the call tool's transcript. You can also import a results CSV and videos from your current testing tool.

    You get one folder per participant under sessions/, with any problems listed by name.

  7. Analyse each session

    Skill: analyse-sessions

    Say to Claude
    Analyse the sessions.

    Each transcript is redacted first. Then Claude writes short observations, one idea each. Every quote must match what the participant said word for word, and every behaviour must point to a logged click or a screenshot. Anything that doesn't check out is dropped, not patched up.

  8. Group findings on the board

    Skill: synthesise-study

    Say to Claude
    Synthesise the study.

    Claude drafts an affinity map from the bottom up, then opens a drag-and-drop board in your browser. Move notes, split, merge and rename groups, and mark the board done. Only then does it write pain points and opportunities, each compared with your product context.

    You get insights with "3 of 5 participants" counts and confidence, all computed in code.

  9. Review and get the report

    Skill: review-findings

    Say to Claude
    Walk me through the findings.

    You accept, edit or reject each insight. Only accepted ones reach the report.

    You get report.md and report.html: findings, task success with 90% intervals, SEQ and SUS scores, the affinity map and questions for further research.

  10. Feed back and re-test

    Skill: update-context · apply-findings · plan-retest

    Ask Claude to update the product context: it proposes before-and-after changes to personas, journeys and the problem statement, each linked to its evidence. Ask it to apply findings: it proposes prototype changes on a branch for you to review. Then plan a re-test with the same tasks word for word, and compare task success before and after.

  11. Clean up

    Skill: cleanup

    Say to Claude
    Run retention cleanup for this study.

    Recordings, screenshots and transcripts are deleted after the study's retention period (90 days by default). To remove one person completely, ask to erase that participant. Claude always shows what will be deleted first.
